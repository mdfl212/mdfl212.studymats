Comprehensive Report: Tests for Common Neurodevelopmental and Behavioral Disorders

Group 5 Reporting  |  Based on Nelson Textbook of Pediatrics, 22nd Edition

I. Introduction

Neurodevelopmental and behavioral disorders represent a significant proportion of childhood morbidity, affecting approximately 15–20% of children and adolescents. These conditions encompass a wide spectrum—from specific learning disabilities and ADHD to autism spectrum disorder (ASD), intellectual disability, and sleep disorders that masquerade as behavioral problems.

The cornerstone of pediatric practice is developmental surveillance and screening. As emphasized in Nelson's Textbook of Pediatrics (22nd ed.), Developmental surveillance is a flexible, longitudinal, continuous process of gathering and synthesizing information about a child's development. The AAP recommends general developmental screening using validated tools at 9, 18, and 30 months of age, with ASD-specific screening at 18 and 24 months.

This report outlines the essential screening tests for common neurodevelopmental and behavioral disorders, structured according to the Philippine Clinical Practice Guidelines aligned with DOH recommendations.

II. Screening Tools by Disorder

A. Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD)

When to Screen: Children ≥4 years with concerns regarding inattention, hyperactivity, or impulsivity persisting ≥6 months, present in ≥2 settings, and causing functional impairment.

Recommended Screening Instruments (Nelson 22nd ed.):

Tool Age Range Description Administration
Vanderbilt ADHD Diagnostic Parent Rating Scale (VADPRS) 4–17 years 55 items assessing DSM-5 criteria, performance, and comorbidities Parent-completed, 10 min
Vanderbilt ADHD Diagnostic Teacher Rating Scale (VADTRS) 4–17 years Parallel teacher version Teacher-completed
Conners Comprehensive Behavior Rating Scales 6–18 years Multi-informant assessment of ADHD and comorbidities Parent/Teacher/Self
SNAP-IV Rating Scale 6–18 years 26 items based on DSM criteria Parent/Teacher
Note: A validated Filipino translation of the Vanderbilt ADHD Parent Rating Scale is available and has demonstrated 100% sensitivity and specificity comparable to the English version.

Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5) per Nelson:

  • ≥6 symptoms of inattention OR ≥6 symptoms of hyperactivity/impulsivity
  • Symptoms present for ≥6 months
  • Several symptoms present before age 12
  • Symptoms present in ≥2 settings
  • Clear evidence of functional impairment

B. Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD)

When to Screen: Universal screening at 18 and 24 months; earlier screening if concerns arise.

Tool Age Range Description Administration
M-CHAT-R/F (Modified Checklist for Autism in Toddlers, Revised with Follow-Up) 16–30 months 20-item parent questionnaire; gold standard first-line screener Parent-completed, 5–10 min
ASQ:SE-2 (Ages & Stages Questionnaires: Social-Emotional) 1–72 months Screens social-emotional development including ASD risk Parent-completed
PDDST-II (Pervasive Developmental Disorders Screening Test-II) 12–48 months Stage 1 for primary care screening Parent-completed
CSBS (Communication and Symbolic Behavior Scales) 6–24 months Infant-toddler checklist for communication and symbolic behavior Parent-completed

Red Flags for ASD (Nelson 22nd ed.):

  • No babbling by 12 months
  • No gesturing (pointing, waving) by 12 months
  • No single words by 16 months
  • No two-word phrases by 24 months
  • Loss of previously acquired language or social skills
  • Poor eye contact, lack of shared interest

C. Developmental Delay / Intellectual Disability

When to Screen: Universal screening at 9, 18, and 30 months; whenever developmental concerns are raised.

Tool Age Range Description Administration
ASQ-3 (Ages & Stages Questionnaire, 3rd Edition) 1–66 months 21 questionnaires covering 5 domains: communication, gross motor, fine motor, problem-solving, personal-social Parent-completed, 10–15 min
MMDST (Metro Manila Developmental Screening Test) 0–6.5 years Philippine-adapted version of Denver II; assesses personal-social, fine motor, language, gross motor Direct administration
Brigance Screens 0–90 months Brief screening of developmental and academic skills Direct/observation
Bayley Scales of Infant Development (BSID-IV) 1–42 months Comprehensive diagnostic assessment (not screening) Direct administration

D. Specific Learning Disorders

When to Screen: School-aged children with academic difficulties despite adequate instruction.

Screening Approach:

  • Academic screening: PHIL-IRI (Philippine Informal Reading Inventory), standardized math/numeracy assessments
  • Cognitive screening: WISC-V (Wechsler Intelligence Scale for Children) for diagnostic purposes
  • Behavioral screening: Conners 3 or BASC-3 to rule out ADHD/emotional factors

E. Sleep Disorders (Behavioral Insomnia of Childhood)

When to Screen: Children with daytime sleepiness, inattention, irritability, or academic decline with suspected sleep disruption.

Screening Instruments:

  • BEARS Sleep Screening Tool: Bedtime, Excessive sleepiness, Awakenings, Regularity, Snoring (5-item brief screen)
  • Childhood Sleep Habits Questionnaire (CSHQ): Parent-completed, 33 items
  • Sleep Diary: 2-week prospective log of sleep-wake patterns

Key Red Flags (Nelson 22nd ed., Chapter 31 – Sleep Medicine):

  • Snoring ≥3 nights/week + daytime sleepiness → evaluate for OSA
  • Prolonged sleep latency (>30 min) with resistance → behavioral insomnia
  • Circadian rhythm disruption from electronic device use

📌 Key Points (Nelson 22nd ed. & First Aid Step 2 CK):
• ADHD diagnosis is clinical — no lab test or imaging confirms it.
• DSM-5 criteria: ≥6 symptoms (≥5 for ≥17 yrs) in either domain, present ≥6 months, onset <12 yrs.
• First-line pharmacotherapy: Stimulants (methylphenidate, amphetamines) — 70–80% response rate.
• Non-pharmacologic: Parent training, behavioral classroom interventions, organizational skills training.
• Always assess for sleep disorders — they can mimic or exacerbate ADHD symptoms.
• Filipino context: Validated Filipino Vanderbilt scales are available for screening.
